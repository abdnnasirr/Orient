# Map

A map holds one understanding so that anyone can carry it. It is orienting, applied to thoughts: it takes the mess, the ideas, the facts, the unknowns, and works them with the owner, cutting, condensing, adding, until both hold one shape: one picture of the whole, the few parts that make it, each part in its place, and the technical under the idea it serves. It is said to one person who was not there, in plain words and in the order a proof is told, this is so, so this follows, so this is what we do, with nothing said for effect. It holds what that person carries away, not what the source contains, so that they walk away able to explain it to someone else, and so that the owner, back after a week, picks up where they left off.

**The frame comes first, and you propose it.** Before anything is made, say what the whole is, the few things it is made of, and how they relate, from the reader's question and not from what was built or who built it. A frame is found when it can be drawn in one picture, the parts in their places and the relation between them visible, and it is the picture that gets the nod, not a sentence about it. If it cannot be drawn, it is not a frame yet. Test it: every item has exactly one home, things side by side are one kind of thing, and what will arrive later has a place waiting. When something new has no home, the frame changes; nothing is appended.

**Sections are the parts of the frame, and only those.** Anything that runs across every part, where it runs, where its code is, what it saves, what it costs, folds under each part; it never becomes a section of its own, because then every part is met once per aspect. Each part is earned before it is named: what is so, what must therefore be, then the thing that meets it. A thing named before its need is a fact to memorize, not a step in a proof.

**Each thing has one place, and earns it.** The idea in one line, then one line per part, then each part opened the same way. A point is made where it belongs and nowhere earlier; the level above says only its point. A piece stays only if the reader could not hold the whole without it; what is interesting but not needed goes. Depth stays where it lives, in the code, the data, the files, and the map points at it. A name is on the map only if the reader will use it; it is given once and pointed at after, the same word everywhere. The whole map is seen at a glance; if it is not, it is holding something that is not the reader's to carry.

**What is open is on the map.** Unknowns and decisions not made are marked where they sit, with what would settle them, never buried and never written as settled.

Done when the reader can place any question in the frame and explain the whole.
